01 / Near the station

The route picks itself.

Save a commute with its hours, or let WhichWay learn the trip you usually make at this time of day, from where you are. Walk toward the station and the route starts on its own: the train to take, the countdown to your platform and the arrival with its 80% window are on screen before you reach for the phone.

  • Commutes apply in their own time window, from a fixed station or the nearest one.
  • No commute on? The trip you usually make at this hour fills in.
  • Walking toward the station, or within 150 m of it, the route begins. No Start button.

02 / The doors close

It knows you’re on the train.

With motion sensing on, the phone feels the train pull away and weighs that moment against every train that just left your platform, with the feed’s own positions. The stops it feels along the way, and where you step off, settle it. It says “On the F”, or “On the G, not the F” when you took the other line.

  • Unsure, or the phone was deep in a bag? It assumes the train you planned, and says “presumably”.
  • One tap on the trains that just left puts it right, and your word stands for the ride.

03 / On the way

The fastest way, from the train you’re on.

Once you’re aboard, the route follows that train, not the plan: the countdown to getting off, the change it makes with its margin and walk, and the arrival as that train will make it, with its window.

  • Took the F when the plan said the R? The route becomes the F’s: change at W 4 St, not Jay St.
  • Stayed on past the change? It re-plans to a later change, or straight there, whichever gets you in first.
  • The same ride sits on your lock screen as a Live Activity.

The model

The prediction model underneath.

Picking your route, naming your train and re-planning on the way all lean on one thing: knowing when every train will really be where. The MTA’s countdown runs about half a minute optimistic, and its feed logs an arrival 30 to 95 seconds after the train pulls in. WhichWay corrects both, with a model trained on three weeks of every train in the system.

  • 67.6 sheld-out arrival error, against 85.5 s for the timetable
  • 43%closer than the MTA countdown, where the countdown was sampled
  • 80%of held-out arrivals land inside the 80% window shown
  • 30–95 show late the feed logs an arrival; WhichWay times the pull-in

A gradient-boosted model, one to twenty stops ahead, with three quantiles for every arrival. It learns how long a held train stays and how lateness carries down the line, and every live forecast is scored against what happened. Figures from the engine’s model report and its platform timing study of October 2026. The whole engine runs on the phone.
